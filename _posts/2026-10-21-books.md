**Fair warning**: this list is a mix of some recent reads I enjoyed and older ones that I can actually remember. My reading speed generally outpaces my website-updating speed, so these additions might be a bit sporadic.
## Books about nature 
- **Journey to the Ants** by  Bert Hölldobler and Edward O. Wilson. This book is so well-written. If you only ever read one book about ants, make it this one.
- **Forest Walking** by Peter Wohlleben
- **Entangled Life: How Fungi make Our Worlds, Change Our Minds & Shape Our Futures** by Merlin Sheldrake

## Books about science and mathematics
- **The New World of Mr. Tompkins** by George Gamow & Russell Stannard. I first read this book 18 years ago and it is still one of my favourite. 
- **The Evolution of Cooperation** by Robert Axelrod. Another incredibly well-written science book. It taught me so much about how to frame the right questions and how to design experiments to answer those questions. 
- **Brilliant Green: The Surprising History and Science of Plant Intelligence** by Stefano Mancuso
- **「物理とはなんだろう」/《物理是什麼》** by 朝永 振一郎 (Shin'ichirō Tomonaga). There isn't an English translation available, but I highly recommend it if you read Japanese or Chinese!

## Books that make you laugh
- **The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy** series by Douglas Adams
- **The Discworld** series by Terry Pratchett. There's a whole library of them out there. Just start with whichever one sounds interesting to you. I started with **Small Gods** and it is absolutely brilliant.
- **The Mother Tongue** by Bill Bryson.

## Books about travel
- **The Travelling Tree/「旅をする木」** by 星野 道夫 (Michio Hoshino)
- **A Walk in the Woods** by Bill Bryson. Currently reading and it's hilarious.
- Travel writing by 村上春樹 (Haruki Murakami). Most of these don't have English translations, but again I highly recommend it if you read Japanese or Chinese. Some good ones are:「雨天炎天」/《雨天炎天》（Greece and Türkiye）,「遠い太鼓」/《遠方的鼓聲》(Greece and Italy), 「辺境 · 近境」/《邊境 · 近境》(America, Mexico, China, Mongolia, Japan).

## Books about people
- **Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman** by Richard Feynman. Another one of my favourite books since high school. 
- **Maker of Patterns** by Freeman Dyson
- **The Calculus of Friendship** by Steven H. Strogatz
- **The Worlds I See: Curiosity, Exploration, and Discovery at the Dawn of AI /《我看見的世界》** by 李飛飛（Fei-Fei Li） 
- **Autobiography** by Bertrand Russell

## Books with good stories
- **The Painted Veil** by W. Somerset Maugham
- **The Alchemist** by Paulo Coelho
- **The Namiya General Store /《解憂雜貨店》** by 東野圭吾 (Keigo Higashino)
- **The City and Its Uncertain Walls** by 村上春樹 (Haruki Murakami)

## Detective novels
I like binge-reading, especially when it comes to detective novels.
- The complete Agatha Christie collection. If you have never read any of them and wonder where to start, I would recommend **Murder on the Orient Express** (an absolute classic) or the **Death on the Nile** or **The Murder of Roger Ackroyd** (she pulled a trick on us in this one!).
- The Nationality Mysteries series by Ellery Queen. I just really like their idea of dropping an interlude before the final chapter to let the readers know that all clues needed to solve the mystery have been presented. My first Ellery Queen novel was **The Dutch Shoe Mystery** (it was a gift from my best friend), but pick whichever one you like.
